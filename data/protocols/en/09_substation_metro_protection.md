# Power Substation and Metro Entrance Protection Protocol

## 1. Purpose

Loss of a substation or flooding of a metro station has effects far beyond the flooded street: traffic signals fail, pumps stop, hospitals switch to generators, and thousands of passengers must be moved. This protocol coordinates the FOC with the electricity and metro operators when a zone containing these assets reaches elevated risk.

## 2. Notification thresholds

2.1. Forecast risk 40 or higher within 24 hours: the FOC sends an information notice to the operators' control rooms.

2.2. Forecast risk 60 or higher within twelve hours: operators activate their flood plans; the FOC confirms which pump units are earmarked for their sites.

2.3. RED alert in the zone: the FOC opens a dedicated coordination channel with each operator for the duration of the event.

## 3. Substations

3.1. Cable basements and transformer bunds are the vulnerable points. Operators confirm sump pumps are working and standby pumps are connected.

3.2. Sandbag or modular barriers are placed at cable basement doors and ventilation openings below one metre.

3.3. If water is reported inside a cable basement, the FOC assigns the nearest available pump unit with RED priority and asks the operator to prepare a controlled switch-over so that load can be transferred before a fault trips the station.

3.4. A substation must never be pumped into a neighbouring underpass; the discharge route is agreed before pumping begins.

## 4. Metro entrances

4.1. Entrances at street level are protected first by flood gates where installed and otherwise by modular barriers, with a clear escape path always maintained.

4.2. When the zone reaches ORANGE, the operator stops escalators to the lower concourse and posts staff at each entrance.

4.3. When the zone reaches RED, or water is within 20 cm of the entrance threshold, the operator closes the entrance and trains run through without stopping. The FOC records the closure and updates the public message.

4.4. Passengers already inside are held on the platform level only if it is dry and ventilated; otherwise they are guided out through the highest entrance.

## 5. Reopening

5.1. Substation systems that were wet are inspected by the operator before re-energising; the FOC does not set the time.

5.2. Metro entrances reopen when the street is below 5 cm of standing water and the concourse drainage has been checked.

## 6. Logging

All notifications, pump assignments and closures are logged in the FOC decision log with the operator contact and the time of acknowledgement.
