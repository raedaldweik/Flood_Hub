# Pre-Storm Drain and Gully Inspection Procedure

## 1. Purpose

A blocked inlet turns a survivable storm into a flood. This procedure defines how public works crews inspect and clear storm drains, gullies and soakaway pits in forecast-risk zones during the window between a forecast and the first rainfall.

## 2. Activation

2.1. The FOC issues an inspection task list when any zone has forecast risk 40 or higher within 24 hours.

2.2. Zones are prioritised by forecast risk, then by criticality, then by the number of underpasses.

2.3. In practice the window is short. Crews aim to complete underpass inlets within six hours of activation and the remainder within twelve.

## 3. Inspection order within a zone

3.1. Underpass sump inlets and their pump chamber screens.

3.2. Inlets at the low points of the main roads listed in the zone file.

3.3. Inlets in front of hospitals, substations and metro entrances.

3.4. Residential street gullies, starting from the lowest streets.

## 4. What to check

4.1. Grating present, seated and free of sediment, leaves, plastic and construction debris. Sand accumulation after a dusty week is the most common blockage.

4.2. The connecting pipe carries water when a bucket is poured; a full sump that does not drain indicates a downstream blockage that requires a jetting crew.

4.3. Soakaway pits are not silted; a pit that holds water for more than an hour after a test pour is reported for maintenance.

4.4. Manhole covers are seated and not displaced by earlier flows.

## 5. Recording

5.1. Each inlet is marked cleared, partially cleared or blocked in the field application, with a photograph.

5.2. The zone summary is sent to the FOC, which lowers the effective drainage capacity used for that zone if more than one in five inlets remain blocked.

## 6. Safety

6.1. Crews work in pairs. No crew member enters a sump or chamber; blockages below the grating are cleared with tools or by a jetting unit.

6.2. Inspection stops when rainfall begins in the zone; crews withdraw to their vehicles and report position.

## 7. After the storm

Every inlet that was reported blocked is re-inspected and cleared before the zone is returned to normal posture. Recurrent blockages are flagged for redesign.
