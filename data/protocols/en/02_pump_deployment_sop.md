# Mobile Pump Deployment Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Scope

This procedure governs the pre-positioning, dispatch, operation and recovery of the mobile pump truck and tanker fleet. The city relies on mobile pumping because fixed storm drainage was designed for a drier climate; during extreme rainfall the fleet is the primary means of removing standing water from streets and underpasses.

## 2. Fleet posture levels

2.1. **Level 0 - Normal.** All units at depots, crews on normal rosters.

2.2. **Level 1 - Standby.** Forecast probability of heavy rain within 24 hours. Crews confirmed, fuel topped up, hoses and strainers inspected.

2.3. **Level 2 - Pre-positioned.** Forecast zone risk of 60 or higher within six hours. Units move to the hotspot staging points listed in the zone file. The target is every underpass zone covered by at least two pump units before rainfall begins.

2.4. **Level 3 - Active pumping.** Zones at ORANGE or RED. Units are pumping, and reallocation is driven by time-to-drain estimates.

## 3. Staging rules

3.1. Stage on high ground within 500 m of the hotspot, never inside the low point itself.

3.2. A staged unit must have a clear exit route that does not pass through the area it is meant to drain.

3.3. Tanker crews are staged alongside pump trucks where there is no gravity outfall, so that pumped water can be hauled away rather than recirculated.

## 4. Allocation principles

4.1. Allocation is proportional to estimated standing water volume, weighted by zone criticality. Hospitals, substations and metro entrances outrank residential streets.

4.2. Total allocations shall never exceed the available fleet. Any proposed plan, including plans proposed by the Rafid assistant, is validated against fleet limits and zone eligibility by rule R-05 before it can be executed.

4.3. A plan becomes executable only when an authorised operator approves it. Assistants and forecasting systems propose; operators decide.

## 5. Operating a pump position

5.1. Confirm the discharge point is a functioning drain, an outfall, or a tanker; never discharge into an adjacent street.

5.2. Fit strainers before starting; debris is the most common cause of pump failure during storms.

5.3. Report pumping rate and observed water level every thirty minutes so that time-to-drain estimates stay current.

## 6. Recovery

6.1. A position is released when standing water is below 5 cm and the inflow has stopped.

6.2. Released units return to depot or are reassigned by the dispatcher, and the release is logged.
